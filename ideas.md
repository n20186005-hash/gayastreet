# Gaya Street Kota Kinabalu — Design Direction

## Three Possible Approaches

### Theme Name: Sunday Market Field Notes
Very brief intro: A tactile travel-journal direction built from dawn light, market awnings, heritage shophouse colors, and editorial field-note annotations. It feels local, observant, and made for walking.
Probability: 0.07

### Theme Name: Borneo Coastal Modernism
Very brief intro: A restrained coastal identity using sea-glass blues, sun-washed neutrals, architectural lines, and generous negative space. It positions Gaya Street as a calm urban gateway to Sabah.
Probability: 0.03

### Theme Name: Night Market Signal
Very brief intro: A dark, electric evening-market direction with warm food-stall accents and luminous wayfinding details. It foregrounds the street after sunset while keeping the atmosphere grounded in place.
Probability: 0.09

## Chosen Approach: Sunday Market Field Notes

### Design Movement
Contemporary editorial travel publishing mixed with Southeast Asian vernacular modernism: documentary photography, hand-marked cartographic cues, sun-faded paper tones, and strong typographic rhythm.

### Core Principles
1. The street is the interface: composition should feel like moving down Jalan Gaya, not browsing a generic card grid.
2. Local texture beats polish: awnings, tiled walks, painted facades, kopi steam, and market hands carry the emotional weight.
3. Information should read like a useful field note: concise, navigable, and grounded in practical details.
4. Every accent must earn its place through wayfinding, not decoration.

### Color Philosophy
Ink blue anchors the site like early morning shade under a five-foot way. Turmeric yellow acts as the Sunday-market signal color, while coral red marks food, movement, and human warmth. Paper cream keeps the page breathable and makes photography feel archival rather than glossy.

### Layout Paradigm
A vertical street walk: an offset hero, a narrow “wayfinding rail” for section labels, alternating left/right content blocks, a pinned practical guide, and a map that behaves like the last stop of the walk. Avoid a uniform centered grid.

### Signature Elements
- A thin route-line motif with numbered stops and small direction ticks.
- Underlined handwritten-style annotations for “field notes” and practical tips.
- Coral/yellow marker tabs that resemble market-stall price tags without pretending to be official signage.

### Interaction Philosophy
Interactions should feel like turning a page or following a street marker. Hover states reveal a little more context, accordions open with a short, decisive movement, and anchor navigation keeps the visitor oriented without taking them away from the single-page walk.

### Animation
Use restrained 180–240ms ease-out transitions. Reveal section notes with a gentle vertical drift, animate route markers by opacity and transform only, and avoid autoplay motion that competes with documentary photography. Respect reduced-motion preferences.

### Typography System
Display: Fraunces, with italic emphasis for lived-in editorial moments. Body: Source Sans 3 for highly readable practical information. Labels: IBM Plex Mono in small uppercase tracking for coordinates, section markers, and metadata.

### Brand Essence
A field guide to Gaya Street for curious walkers who want the market, the stories, and the practical details in one place. Personality: observant, warm, grounded.

### Brand Voice
Headlines are compact and sensory. CTAs are direct invitations rather than sales language. Microcopy is specific and useful.

Example lines:
- “Follow the street where Sunday starts early.”
- “Save this stop for the first cup of kopi.”

### Wordmark & Logo
Use a compact symbol combining a palm leaf, a shophouse roofline, and a rising sun arc. The wordmark sits beside the symbol in a narrow uppercase sans, with the mark remaining recognizable when reduced to a favicon.

### Signature Brand Color
Market Turmeric: #E6A92B — an ownable yellow drawn from sunlit awnings, kuih, and hand-painted stall markers.
